Multivariate Confirmation Analysis: Disconnection Persistence Among Non–Postsecondary Graduates

Introduction

The bivariate analysis of workforce disconnection among non-college graduates identified two distinct disconnection mechanisms operating within a single combined measure. Non-sustained employment (working, but below the sustained-employment threshold) and absence of any Minnesota employment record behave as structurally different phenomena: the former is front-loaded and largely resolves within five years, while the latter is bimodal and behaves as a durable end-state once established. Separating these two mechanisms revealed that different demographic and high-school-accomplishment variables predict each one — most notably, Special Education status and English learner status appeared to drive no-record persistence specifically, while Economic status and Gender appeared to drive non-sustained persistence specifically, and different racial/ethnic groups showed disparities concentrated in one mechanism or the other.

This multivariate analysis exists to confirm whether that mechanism-split pattern holds once all variables are considered simultaneously, rather than one at a time. This step is necessary because earlier work in this project has shown that bivariate relationships — particularly those involving English learner status — can substantially attenuate or shift once other variables, especially missing-data patterns in academic assessment scores, are accounted for in the same model. Before this mechanism-split finding is presented as a confirmed result, it should be tested under the same conditions.

Methodology

Study Population

The analytic sample is restricted to individuals who did not complete a postsecondary credential, consistent with the non-college-graduate population used throughout this project. The sample is further restricted to individuals who graduated (or exited postsecondary attendance, for those who attended without completing) after 2010, consistent with the cohort restriction applied elsewhere in this project to avoid the PSEO participation tracking gap present in earlier cohorts.

Dependent Variables

This analysis models two outcomes, each derived from the disconnection-persistence categories established in the bivariate analysis. Each graduate’s workforce status was tracked across the first five years after exiting formal education (high school for direct-to-workforce graduates, or postsecondary attendance for those who attended without completing).

  • Persistent non-sustained employment — binary (1 = non-sustained workforce participation in 4 or 5 of the first 5 years, 0 = otherwise). This isolates the instability mechanism identified in the bivariate work.
  • Persistent no-record status — binary (1 = no Minnesota employment record in 4 or 5 of the first 5 years, 0 = otherwise). This isolates the disappearance-from-the-system mechanism identified in the bivariate work.

Each outcome is modeled separately, in its own specification, rather than as levels of a single combined variable. This mirrors the logic of the bivariate analysis: combining the two mechanisms into one model would risk re-obscuring the distinction this analysis is specifically designed to confirm.

Independent Variables

The following indepdent variables were confirmed associated to our dependent variables in the bivariate analysis. These will all be considered for use in the modeling.

Demographics

  • Gender
  • RaceEthnicity
  • economic.status
  • SpecialEdStatus
  • english.learner

High school characteristics

  • Dem_Desc
  • edr
  • avg.unemp.rate
  • avg.wages.pct.state

High school accomplishments

  • pseo.participant
  • ap.exam
  • took.ACT
  • ACTCompositeScore (recoded to 0 for non-test-takers, entered alongside took.ACT, per the missing-indicator approach established earlier in this project)
  • cte.achievement
  • total.cte.courses.taken
  • sat.taken

Postsecondary variable

  • attended.ps — included specifically because it was the single strongest predictor of overall disconnection in the bivariate analysis, and it is the one variable found to meaningfully affect both disconnection mechanisms rather than only one.

Modeling Approach

Each outcome (persistent non-sustained status, persistent no-record status) is modeled using a staged block-entry sequence, consistent with the modeling structure used throughout this project’s other multivariate confirmation analyses. Predictors are introduced in four stages to make attenuation of any individual variable’s effect visible and attributable to a specific block, rather than only observable in a single final specification.

Model 1: Demographics

  • Demographics: Gender, RaceEthnicity, economic.status, SpecialEdStatus, english.learner,

Model 2: add High School Characteristics

  • High school characteristics: Dem_Desc, edr, avg.unemp.rate, avg.wages.pct.state

Model 3: Add High School Accomplishments

  • Adds: pseo.participant, took.ACT, ACTCompositeScore.filled, ap.exam, cte.achievement, total.cte.courses.taken, sat.taken

Model 4: Add Postsecondary Attempt

  • Adds: attended.ps

Each model is compared to the prior one using a likelihood ratio test to confirm it adds significant explanatory power, with AIC tracked across the sequence to summarize overall model improvement. This staged structure is applied identically to both outcomes, allowing direct comparison of how each mechanism’s predictors evolve across the same three blocks.

Evaluation Criteria

Results will be reported using odds ratios, 95% confidence intervals, and statistical significance levels. Interpretation will focus specifically on whether the demographic variables identified as mechanism-specific in the bivariate analysis (Special Education and English learner status for no-record persistence; Economic status and Gender for non-sustained persistence; racial/ethnic groups for whichever mechanism their bivariate disparity was concentrated in) remain significant, attenuate, or reverse once the full variable set is included. Findings will be described as associations holding other included variables constant. No causal claims will be made.


Prepping the data

The modeling dataset begins from the merged file containing both the disconnection-persistence categories (established in the bivariate analysis) and the full set of demographic, high school, and postsecondary variables used throughout this project. Three preparation steps are applied before modeling.

Cohort restriction. The sample is restricted to graduates from 2011 onward (grad.year > 2010). This mirrors the cohort restriction applied throughout this project’s other analyses of non-college graduates: PSEO participation was not reliably tracked prior to 2011 (missingness runs 92-100% in the 2008-2010 cohorts, dropping to near-zero by 2014), and including those cohorts would risk misclassifying untracked PSEO participants as confirmed non-participants.

Reference level assignment. Categorical variables are releveled to match the reference categories used consistently throughout this project (male, White, non-economically-disadvantaged, non-Special-Education, non-English-learner, EDR 5 - North Central, no CTE involvement, non-participant/non-test-taker for the binary accomplishment variables). This keeps odds ratios in this analysis directly comparable in direction and interpretation to every other model built in this project.

Missing-indicator recodes for two variables with structural or correlated missingness:

  • ACT composite score. Students who did not take the ACT have no composite score by definition — confirmed here to be a fully deterministic relationship with took.ACT (zero mismatches between the two). ACTCompositeScore is recoded to 0 for non-test-takers and entered into the model alongside took.ACT, so that took.ACT absorbs the effect of test participation itself while the filled score captures the achievement gradient among test-takers only.

Dependent variable construction. The two outcomes tested in this analysis are built directly from the bivariate persistence categories, collapsed to binary indicators:

  • persistent.non_sustained — 1 if the graduate was non-sustained in 4 or 5 of their first 5 years post-education-exit, 0 otherwise.

  • persistent.no_record — 1 if the graduate had no Minnesota employment record in 4 or 5 of those years, 0 otherwise.

Gender and Race/Ethnicity

  • Female graduates show significantly higher odds of persistent non-sustained employment (OR = 1.54, 95% CI 1.42–1.67) relative to male graduates — consistent in direction with the bivariate finding that Gender’s disconnection effect concentrated specifically in employment instability.

Summary: Multivariate Confirmation — Persistent Non-Sustained Employment

Overview

This analysis modeled persistent non-sustained employment (4–5 of the first 5 years post-exit) using a four-block sequence — demographics, HS characteristics, HS accomplishments, postsecondary attendance — to test whether the mechanism-split pattern identified in the bivariate analysis holds once all variables are considered simultaneously. Sample: N = 21,384 (2011+ cohorts). Final pseudo-R² = 0.0294, modest in absolute terms but consistent with the low overall explanatory power seen throughout this project’s confirmatory (not predictive) framing.

Model Sequence

Model 1 (Demographics): AIC 16,510.06 | Pseudo R² 0.0210 | — (baseline) Model 2 (+ HS characteristics): AIC 16,509.37 | Pseudo R² 0.0216 | χ²(5) = 10.69, p = 0.058 (marginal, not significant) Model 3 (+ HS accomplishments): AIC 16,471.76 | Pseudo R² 0.0248 | χ²(8) = 53.61, p < 0.001 Model 4 (+ Postsecondary attendance): AIC 16,396.03 | Pseudo R² 0.0294 | χ²(1) = 77.73, p < 0.001

HS characteristics is the only block in this sequence that failed to clear conventional significance as a set — consistent with the bivariate finding that HS-context variables showed weak-to-negligible relationships with non-sustained employment specifically.

Variables Retained (Final Model 4)

Demographics: Gender, RaceEthnicity, economic.status, SpecialEdStatus, english.learner HS characteristics: Dem_Desc, edr, avg.wages.pct.state, avg.unemp.rate HS accomplishments: pseo.participant, took.ACT, ACTCompositeScore.filled, ap.exam, sat.taken, cte.achievement, total.cte.courses.taken Postsecondary: attended.ps

Headline Findings

  1. SpecialEdStatus is the dominant predictor of persistent non-sustained employment, and this is a genuine revision to the bivariate framing. Rather than the “mixed, evenly-split-between-mechanisms” pattern suggested by the bivariate work, SpecialEdStatus strengthens across the first three blocks (OR = 1.81 → 1.81 → 1.94) before a slight pullback in the final model (OR = 1.87) — remaining the single largest coefficient in every model of this sequence. This should be treated as substantially non-sustained-driven once other variables are controlled, not as an evenly-mixed mechanism.

  2. RaceEthnicityBlack shows a large, non-attenuating, and ultimately strengthening association with persistent non-sustained employment (OR = 1.71 → 1.73 → 1.72 → 1.81 across the four models) — this disparity does not get explained away by any demographic, HS-context, academic, or postsecondary variable tested. This is a meaningful departure from the bivariate characterization that Black graduates’ disparity was “roughly evenly split” between the two mechanisms; the multivariate result suggests non-sustained employment specifically carries a substantial, independent share of that disparity. Whether the no-record model shows a comparably strong effect will determine how much revision this earlier framing needs.

  3. Gender and Economic status both confirm their bivariate mechanism assignment. Both remain significant and stable across all four models (Gender: OR ≈ 1.50–1.54; Economic status: OR ≈ 1.17–1.21), consistent with the bivariate finding that both operate primarily through employment instability rather than record-absence.

  4. English learner status confirms its bivariate mechanism assignment in the opposite direction — non-significant in every single model of this sequence (p ranging from 0.16 to 0.35), consistent with the bivariate finding that this variable’s disconnection effect ran almost entirely through no-record status, not employment instability.

  5. took.ACT and ACTCompositeScore.filled both confirm their bivariate mechanism assignment. Neither reaches conventional significance in any model (ACTCompositeScore reaches only marginal significance, p = 0.074, in the final model) — consistent with the bivariate finding that these two variables’ disconnection relationship ran through record-absence, not employment instability. This is one of the cleanest confirmations of the mechanism-split hypothesis in this entire analysis.

  6. attended.ps is a large, significant, protective predictor (OR = 0.66 in the final model) — individuals who attended postsecondary education without completing show substantially lower odds of persistent non-sustained employment. This confirms attended.ps’s status as the one variable meaningfully affecting both mechanisms, as anticipated in the methodology.

  7. ap.exam shows a persistent, counterintuitive positive association with persistent non-sustained employment (OR ≈ 1.27–1.29 across Models 3–4) — AP exam participants show higher, not lower, odds of employment instability, confirming the same counterintuitive direction found in the bivariate work.

Summary: Multivariate Confirmation — Persistent No Minnesota Employment Record

Overview

This analysis modeled persistent no-record status (4–5 of the first 5 years post-exit with no Minnesota employment record) using the same four-block sequence as the parallel non-sustained analysis — demographics, HS characteristics, HS accomplishments, postsecondary attendance. Sample: N = 21,384. Final pseudo-R² = 0.0505 — notably higher than the parallel non-sustained model’s final pseudo-R² (0.0294), meaning this outcome is more predictable overall from the variables tested here.

Model 1 (Demographics): AIC 20,940.48 | Pseudo R² 0.0149 | — (baseline) Model 2 (+ HS characteristics): AIC 20,908.60 | Pseudo R² 0.0169 | χ²(5) = 41.88, p < 0.001 Model 3 (+ HS accomplishments): AIC 20,397.97 | Pseudo R² 0.0417 | χ²(8) = 526.63, p < 0.001 Model 4 (+ Postsecondary attendance): AIC 20,214.07 | Pseudo R² 0.0505 | χ²(1) = 185.91, p < 0.001

Every block reached significance in this sequence, in contrast to the parallel non-sustained model, where HS characteristics was only marginal (p = 0.058). Both the HS-accomplishments block (χ² = 526.63 vs. 53.61) and the postsecondary-attendance block (χ² = 185.91 vs. 77.73) produced far larger improvements here than in the non-sustained sequence — this outcome is substantially more sensitive to academic and postsecondary variables overall.

Headline Findings

  1. took.ACT is the single largest and most decisive finding in this entire multivariate stage. ACT test-takers show 69% lower odds of persistent no-record status (OR = 0.31 in the final model) — a massive, stable effect across Models 3–4. This stands in complete contrast to the parallel non-sustained model, where took.ACT was never significant at any stage. This is the cleanest possible confirmation of the bivariate mechanism-split hypothesis found anywhere in this project.

  2. ACTCompositeScore.filled confirms the “two forces” pattern specifically within the no-record mechanism. Significant and positive (OR = 1.04 per point) — among test-takers, higher scores predict higher, not lower, odds of persistent no-record status. Combined with took.ACT’s strong protective effect, this replicates the participation-vs-score divergence documented throughout this entire project, now localized specifically to the record-absence pathway.

  3. English learner status is confirmed as a robust, stable, no-record-specific predictor. Significant and large (OR ≈ 1.66–1.68) in every one of the four models, essentially unchanged across the full sequence — while remaining null throughout the entire parallel non-sustained sequence. This is a full, clean confirmation of the bivariate finding.

  4. SpecialEdStatus’s earlier “mixed mechanism” characterization is decisively overturned. Significant but modest in Models 1–2 (OR ≈ 1.19–1.20), it loses significance entirely once HS accomplishments are added in Model 3 (OR = 1.05, p = 0.322) and remains null through Model 4 (OR = 1.00, p = 0.967). Combined with SpecialEdStatus’s strong, stable, strengthening effect throughout the entire non-sustained sequence, this variable should now be described as substantially non-sustained-driven, with no independent no-record contribution once accomplishments are controlled — a direct correction to the original bivariate framing.

  5. RaceEthnicity splits into genuinely distinct group-level patterns, refining the single bivariate “RaceEthnicity” finding into two separate stories. American Indian graduates show the largest effect in this sequence (OR = 2.23 in the final model) and were never significant in the parallel non-sustained model — their disparity is concentrated almost entirely in no-record status. Black graduates show a real but comparatively smaller effect here (OR = 1.36) against a much larger effect in the non-sustained sequence (OR = 1.81) — their disparity runs disproportionately through employment instability. Asian/Pacific Islander and Hispanic graduates fall in between, both significant here (OR ≈ 1.40 each) though smaller than American Indian’s.

  6. economic.status shows a genuine sign reversal across the two mechanisms. Significant and negative here (OR = 0.66 in the final model) — economically disadvantaged graduates show lower odds of persistent no-record status — versus significant and positive in the parallel non-sustained model (OR ≈ 1.20). This is not simply “mixed”; it is actively opposing, and should be described as such: economically disadvantaged non-completers appear more likely to experience unstable employment, but are simultaneously more likely, not less, to remain visible in Minnesota’s tracked employment system while doing so.

  7. avg.wages.pct.state emerges as a genuine no-record-specific predictor, significant and positive across all three blocks where tested (OR = 2.76 → 1.76 → 2.01 across Models 2–4), while remaining null throughout the entire non-sustained sequence. Higher local wages relative to the state average predict higher odds of persistent no-record status — a new finding not established at the bivariate stage, though the standard rescaling caveat applies to this variable’s odds ratio magnitude.

  8. total.cte.courses.taken and cte.achievement both show meaningfully larger protective effects here than in the non-sustained sequence, where they were weak or non-significant. CTE engagement’s overall protective effect against disconnection is disproportionately a no-record phenomenon.

  9. attended.ps confirms its dual-mechanism status, but with a clearly larger effect here. OR = 0.57 in the final no-record model vs. OR = 0.66 in the final non-sustained model — postsecondary exposure, even without completion, is more strongly protective against disappearing from Minnesota’s employment record than against experiencing unstable employment.

  10. sat.taken reverses direction entirely between the two mechanisms. Protective in the non-sustained model (OR ≈ 0.61–0.65) but a risk factor here (OR ≈ 1.76–1.87) — a striking, specific reversal worth flagging clearly, though the small subgroup size (n = 279 SAT-takers project-wide) warrants continued caution.

Diagnostics & Data Quality Notes

VIF: took.ACT and ACTCompositeScore.filled show elevated VIFs (≈3.7–4.0) throughout, somewhat higher than in the parallel non-sustained sequence, expected and structural. total.cte.courses.taken shows a modestly elevated VIF (≈2.0) in Models 3–4, still within acceptable range.

Bottom Line

This sequence provides the clearest, most decisive multivariate confirmation of the bivariate mechanism-split hypothesis found anywhere in this project. took.ACT, ACTCompositeScore.filled, english.learner, and avg.wages.pct.state all show large, stable, no-record-specific effects that are null or negligible in the parallel non-sustained sequence — a clean, mirror-image confirmation of the earlier bivariate work. Two findings required genuine revision rather than simple confirmation: SpecialEdStatus, previously “mixed,” is now confirmed as substantially non-sustained-driven with no independent no-record effect; and RaceEthnicity, previously treated as one variable with an “evenly split” disparity, is now shown to comprise at least two genuinely distinct group-level stories — American Indian graduates’ disparity concentrated in no-record status, Black graduates’ disparity concentrated in non-sustained employment. economic.status and sat.taken both show outright sign reversals across the two mechanisms rather than simple attenuation, the most striking finding being economic.status’s opposing directions, which should be described precisely as such in any final report rather than smoothed into a single “mixed” characterization.

Description of the Analysis and Findings

This analysis tested, via parallel four-block logistic regression sequences (demographics → high school characteristics → high school accomplishments → postsecondary attempt), whether the two disconnection mechanisms identified in the bivariate work — persistent non-sustained employment and persistent no-record status — remain genuinely distinct once all variables are considered simultaneously. Both models were fit on the same analytic population (N = 21,384 non-college graduates, 2011+ cohorts), using the same 17-variable candidate set with identical block structure.

The results confirm the mechanism split clearly, with a similar refinement to what a simple “two separate systems” framing would suggest. Of the variables tested, most sorted into a single dominant mechanism, but several — Race/Ethnicity (specifically Black), economic status, sat.taken, and Hispanic ethnicity — show real, significant relationships with both outcomes, and in three of those four cases, the relationships run in opposite directions.

Hispanic ethnicity and economic.status are the two clearest “opposite direction” findings in this analysis. Hispanic graduates show significantly lower odds of persistent non-sustained employment (OR = 0.80) but significantly higher odds of persistent no-record status (OR = 1.40) — the same demographic characteristic pushing in opposite directions depending on which mechanism is measured. Economic status shows an identical shape: harmful for non-sustained employment (OR = 1.20) and protective for no-record status (OR = 0.66). Combining these two outcomes into a single blended measure, as the original disconnection framing did, would not just dilute these relationships — it would partially cancel them out.

Gender and English learner status are the cleanest single-mechanism confirmations. Gender is significant and harmful for non-sustained employment in every model and completely null for no-record status in every model. English learner status shows the exact mirror pattern — null for non-sustained, significant and harmful for no-record. Neither variable shows any hint of leaking into the other mechanism.

American Indian and Black graduates’ disparities, treated as a single “RaceEthnicity” finding at the bivariate stage, resolve into two genuinely distinct group-level stories. American Indian graduates show no relationship with non-sustained employment at any model stage, but the single largest effect found in either sequence for no-record status (OR = 2.23). Black graduates show the opposite concentration: a large, stable effect for non-sustained employment (OR = 1.81) and a real but considerably smaller effect for no-record status (OR = 1.36). Treating “racial disparity in disconnection” as one undifferentiated story would obscure that these two groups face meaningfully different versions of it.

SpecialEdStatus’s bivariate “mixed mechanism” characterization does not survive full multivariate control. It remains large, stable, and even strengthens across blocks for non-sustained employment (OR = 1.87 in the final model), but its modest bivariate association with no-record status disappears entirely once HS accomplishments are added — a clean example of a relationship that looked mixed at the bivariate stage but resolves to a single dominant mechanism once properly controlled.

took.ACT and ACTCompositeScore.filled deliver the cleanest mechanism-specific confirmation in the entire analysis. took.ACT shows no relationship with non-sustained employment (p = 0.677) but a massive protective effect against no-record status (OR = 0.31) — the single strongest protective finding in either sequence. ACTCompositeScore.filled is only marginal for non-sustained employment but clearly significant and harmful for no-record status, replicating the “engagement helps, performance hurts” tension documented throughout this project, localized specifically to the record-absence pathway.

sat.taken is the third clear opposite-direction finding. Protective for non-sustained employment (OR = 0.61) but harmful for no-record status (OR = 1.76) — a striking reversal, though the small underlying subgroup (n = 279 SAT-takers project-wide) warrants continued caution about the precision of both estimates.

CTE engagement splits cleanly along categorical vs. continuous lines, just as the bivariate work suggested it might. cte.achievement’s Concentrator/Completor category is protective for both mechanisms; the Participant category is protective for no-record only. total.cte.courses.taken resolves to a no-record-only finding — protective and significant there, null for non-sustained employment. Treating “CTE” as a single undifferentiated protective factor would obscure this split.

Two variables show a parallel, independently-replicated pattern worth flagging on its own. attended.ps is strongly protective for both outcomes, with a somewhat larger effect for no-record status (OR = 0.57 vs. 0.66) — confirming the bivariate finding that this was the one variable operating through both channels. avg.unemp.rate becomes only marginal for both outcomes in the final model, having been attenuated in both sequences specifically once attended.ps entered — the same dynamic occurring independently in two separately-run models, suggesting unemployment’s apparent bivariate effect substantially ran through postsecondary attendance rather than reflecting an independent local-economy effect.

avg.wages.pct.state and Dem_Desc both show genuine mechanism-specific regional signatures. avg.wages.pct.state is null for non-sustained employment but significant and harmful for no-record status (OR = 2.01, rescaling caveat applies) — higher local wages predict a higher risk of disappearing from the employment record, consistent with the migration-related interpretation developed throughout this project. Dem_Desc splits by category: “Urban/town/rural mix” matters only for non-sustained employment, while “Entirely urban” matters only for no-record status — different HS-context categories are relevant to different mechanisms, not a single unified urbanicity effect.

The Story the Data Tells

Coming into this analysis, the working assumption — built from the bivariate stage — was that “disconnection” among non-college graduates might really be two problems wearing one label. This multivariate confirmation supports that assumption, but with an important refinement: it isn’t a clean, symmetric split where each variable belongs entirely to one mechanism or the other. A meaningful subset of variables — Hispanic ethnicity, economic status, sat.taken, and to a lesser extent Black identity and SpecialEdStatus — matter for both mechanisms, and for three of them, in genuinely opposite directions. The real finding isn’t “two separate systems with no overlap.” It’s that combining these two outcomes into a single measure would, for Hispanic ethnicity, economic status, and sat.taken specifically, actively cancel out real relationships rather than merely diluting them.

If you are a Hispanic non-completer, the data says both mechanisms apply to you, but in opposite ways: you face a somewhat lower risk of unstable-but-visible employment, even as you face a meaningfully elevated risk of disappearing from Minnesota’s employment record entirely. If you are economically disadvantaged, the same opposing pattern holds. If your risk factor is Gender or English-learner status specifically, only one mechanism applies to you at all — the other shows no relationship whatsoever. If you are American Indian, your elevated disconnection risk is concentrated almost entirely in disappearing from the record, not in unstable employment; if you are Black, the reverse is true.

This has a direct, practical implication for anyone designing an intervention around this population. Programs built around ACT participation specifically would show large, measurable results against record-disappearance, with essentially no effect on job stability. Programs built around CTE achievement level would show real, roughly balanced protective results against both problems, while programs built around CTE course volume alone would only move the record-disappearance risk. Programs built around postsecondary attendance — even incomplete attendance — would help with both, more than almost anything else tested, with a modestly larger benefit for keeping people visible in the state’s employment system. And any intervention built around a single, undifferentiated theory of “workforce disconnection” risks helping one population while doing little for another, or — in the case of economic status and Hispanic ethnicity specifically — appearing to have no effect at all in a pooled analysis, when in fact it is quietly helping with one problem while making the other slightly worse.
