Multiple Correspondence Analysis

master.original <- read.csv("Data/SLEDS/Masters/Master.csv") %>%
  drop_na(Dem_Desc)


The purpose of multiple correspondence analysis is to examine any patterns among the independent variables with the goal of eliminating variables that are redundant - essentially two variables that are explaining the same thing. This type of analysis is not meant to confirm any predictions, but rather explore the data and make decisions on what data should be included in the prediction model.


Independent variables

The master dataset is composed of 122,938 rows. Each row represents an individual that graduated (StatusEnd = 8 or 9) from a high school located in the Central region (defined by project) between 2008 and 2023.

The master dataset is also composed of 113 columns. Columns mainly represent various socio-economic, demographic, and educational career characteristics of the individual as well as a few characteristics of the high school geographic from which they graduated (i.e. county unemployment). The variables for each individual is composed of a mix of categorical and numerical values. In this analysis, we will only be exploring categorical data.

Here is a list of all the categorical variables with a description of each.

Frequencies

First, lets check to see there aren’t any variable categories with very low frequencies which can distort the analysis.

VARIABLES TO ELIMINATE OUTRIGHT

  1. HomelessIndicator:
    • Only 0.9% of graduates (n=1,136) were ever identified as homeless.
    • Too sparse for meaningful MCA contribution or stable bivariate estimates.
    • Eliminate
  2. sat.taken
    • Only 1.8% of graduates (n=2,191) took the SAT.
    • Far too rare to distinguish from noise. Not part of the core framework.
    • Eliminate.

Variables to consolidate

  • ps.grad.InstitutionSector: everal categories need collapsing before MCA
    • Sectors 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 are effectively empty (n=7, 52, 3, 1, 60 respectively).
    • Sector 3 (private for-profit 4-year) is marginal at n=939 (0.8%).
    • NA has only n=3.
    • The new categories will be the following;
      • 1 - Public, 4-year or above
      • 2 - Private not-for-profit, 4-year or above
      • 4 - Public, 2-year
      • 10 - multiple sectors (if attended multiple institutions)
      • 11 - Other type of college
        • 3 - Private for-profit, 4-year or above
        • 5 - Private not-for-profit, 2-year
        • 6 - Private for-profit, 2-year
        • 7 - Public, less-than 2-year
        • 8 - Private not-for-profit, less-than 2-year
        • 9 - Private for-profit, less-than 2-year
        • 99 - Sector unknown (not active)
      • “Never attended ps” = the individual never attended a post-secondary institution
      • “Did not grad” = the individual attended a post-secondary institution but did not graduate.
  • ps.grad.location — “PS grad EDR” category is very small and I will consolidate those so they just read “PS grad region”
  • highest.cred.level: I will collapse some of these categories and have the following;
    • Did not graduate ps
    • Less than a bachelor’s
    • Bachelor’s or higher

VARIABLES TO WATCH BUT KEEP

  • RaceEthnicity/AI: n=642 (0.5%). Small but present in the framework; retain for bivariate stages with appropriate caution in interpretation. May be unstable in multivariate models.
  • LimitedEnglishProficiencyIndicator: n=2,625 (2.1%) identified as Y. Small but not negligible. Keep for now; its relationship to english.learner (8.9%) and non.english.home (8.8%) will be assessed in the MCA.

Several categories show notably low total cos² across five dimensions and warrant attention:

SUMMARY AND RECOMMENDATIONS

VARIABLES ELIMINATED PRIOR TO MCA

Two variables were eliminated based on frequency review before the MCA was conducted. HomelessIndicator was dropped due to extremely low prevalence (0.9%), and sat.taken was dropped due to both low prevalence (1.8%) and its absence from the core analytical framework.

DEMOGRAPHIC VARIABLES

The demographic MCA produced ten dimensions, with Dimension 1 explaining 27.4% of variance. No single dominant structure emerged, indicating that demographic variables capture several relatively independent axes of variation. Dimension 1 reflected a language/poverty axis driven by non.english.home, english.learner, and economic.status. Dimension 2 was dominated by SpecialEdStatus. Dimensions 3 and 4 captured racial/ethnic variation. Notably, the three language-related variables — english.learner, non.english.home, and LimitedEnglishProficiencyIndicator — loaded on different dimensions despite their conceptual overlap, suggesting they capture meaningfully distinct subpopulations. All demographic variables showed adequate cos² representation. No eliminations are warranted.

Recommendation: Retain all demographic variables. Consistent with the established framework, non.english.home will be excluded from multivariate models due to redundancy with english.learner, but retained for bivariate stages.

HIGH SCHOOL CHARACTERISTICS

With only two variables (Dem_Desc and edr), three dimensions exhausted all variance. Dem_Desc drove Dimension 2, reflecting urban/rural character, while edr drove Dimensions 1 and 3, reflecting EDR membership. The two variables capture distinct geographic axes with no redundancy. Recommendation: Retain both variables.

HIGH SCHOOL ACCOMPLISHMENTS

Dimension 1 explained 25.5% of variance across six dimensions. took.ACT anchored Dimension 1 as a general academic testing axis. ap.exam and pseo.participant defined an advanced academic coursework axis across Dimensions 2 and 5. cte.achievement dominated Dimensions 3 and 4, with the absence of CTE engagement (No CTE) standing particularly apart. All variables showed adequate cos² representation.

Recommendation: Retain all variables.

POSTSECONDARY VARIABLES

The postsecondary MCA revealed the expected structural redundancy across attended.ps, ps.grad, highest.cred.level, ps.grad.location, and ps.grad.InstitutionSector — these variables are overlapping by construction. Dimension 1 captured non-participation, Dimension 2 captured current enrollment (appearing identically across four variables), Dimension 3 captured credential level and institution type, and Dimension 4 was an artifact of missing data rather than a substantive structure. Several ps.grad.InstitutionSector categories showed weak cos² representation, particularly the collapsed other category (11, total = 0.024) and multiple sectors (10, total = 0.071). ps.grad.location graduation destination categories were also moderately weak.

Recommendation: Retain all postsecondary variables for bivariate stages. The confirmed redundancy reinforces the framework decision to consolidate these variables into the ps.pathway combined variable for multivariate modeling rather than entering them simultaneously. The weak representation of ps.grad.InstitutionSector collapsed categories should be noted when interpreting bivariate results for that variable.

VARIABLES TO CARRY FORWARD WITH CAUTION

RaceEthnicity/AI (n=642, 0.5%) should be retained but interpreted cautiously in bivariate stages and may be unstable in multivariate models. LimitedEnglishProficiencyIndicator (n=2,625, 2.1%) will be retained and its relationship to english.learner and non.english.home monitored throughout the bivariate analysis.
